Bengal's Amavasya night glows differently. While much of India celebrates Diwali, West Bengal erupts for Kali Puja — the night the Dark Mother walks among us.

In temples along the Hooghly, she arrives as Bama Kali: serene, bridal, blessing devotees with peace and sweet offerings.

But Ma is never only one story. At the cremation ground — the shashan — Shashan Kali reigns: terrible, compassionate, destroying ego so truth can breathe.

In North Kolkata's lanes, Phatakeshto Kali rides youth and rebellion — bold, street-side, fiercely loved at midnight feasts.

Chamunda rides the war-torn field. Siddheshwari whispers in old North Calcutta homes. At Adyapeeth, Adya Ma holds centuries of tantra. Rakta Kali marks triumph over evil.

Every para builds her from bamboo and clay. Artisans paint the third eye until she seems to blink. Then the night breaks open — dhak drums, conch shells, lamps, sindoor in the air.

Different names. Different altars. One Bengal. One Mother. Jay Ma Kali!
